Honours Thesis

Healing the History: A Robust Estimation Framework for Addressing Individual Misidentification in POPAN Capture-Recapture Models

Adam Wong-Toi (BSc Hons)
Supervisor: Prof. Rachel Fewster
Deptartment of Statistics, UoA

1. The Problem: Citizen Science & "Ghost" Histories

Following 20th-century commercial whaling, Southern Hemisphere humpback whale populations have experienced a rapid demographic recovery. Modern crowdsourced databases like Happywhale aggregate millions of fluke photos matched using computer vision algorithms across decades, and many different marine species..

However, automated matching is imperfect. When a photograph of a previously sighted individual fails to match its true profile, a misidentification error occurs. This error splits a true multi-sighting profile into two or more fragmented profiles, generating artificial single-sighting records known as "ghosts".

Example: Ghost Generation

True Individual Profile (Whale A):
4 Year Encounter History: [ 1, 0, 1, 1 ] (Sighted in Years 1, 3, and 4)
Observed History (Misidentification in Year 3):
Original Whale A: [ 1, 0, 0, 1 ] (Year 3 sighting dropped)
"Ghost" Whale B:  [ 0, 0, 1, 0 ] (Artificial singleton created!)

These artificial singletons severely inflate open-population capture-recapture abundance parameters because likelihood estimators interpret every ghost as an entirely new, unrepeatable member of the superpopulation.

2. Simulation Studies & Bias Quantification

To benchmark the impact of ghosts, simulation studies were constructed using parameters derived from Happywhale photo quality metrics (a baseline per-photo misidentification rate of $\approx 1.5\%$, translating to a pairwise error rate of $\sim 3\%$ for quality $3+$ images).

Key Simulation Findings across POPAN Formulations:

  • Abundance Overestimation ($N^*$): In standard POPAN finite-mixture heterogeneity models (Capmix), uncorrected misidentification generated an artificial superpopulation inflation of $+7.2\%$.
  • Parameter Resilience ($\phi, \lambda$): Interestingly, apparent survival ($\phi \approx 99\%$) and recruitment/growth ($\lambda \approx 10\%$) parameters remained robust to ghost contamination, as the overall geometric progression of cohort decay across years was preserved.

3. Recovering Latent Sightings: The Saddlepoint Breakthrough

To fix multi-year migration histories, we first needed to model how frequently whales are sighted within a single season. However, the observed encounter count ($Y$) is a corrupted version of the true latent frequency ($X$) due to singleton ghost encounters. Modeling this latent process requires evaluating zero-truncated compound likelihood distributions—a massive computational bottleneck on large datasets.

The Bottleneck

Prohibitive Computation

Standard Monte Carlo integration or numerical convolutions become impossibly slow when fitting complex compound distributions across tens of thousands of Happywhale individual histories.

The Solution

Saddlepoint Density Fitting

By approximating the likelihood directly through the cumulant generating function $K(s)$, saddlepoint methods evaluated exact parameter likelihoods in milliseconds rather than hours.

Using this saddlepoint framework, we benchmarked four candidate distributions (Poisson, Negative Binomial, Geometric, and Discretised Log-Normal).

Optimal Model: Discretised Log-Normal Distribution

The discretised log-normal distribution provided the best statistical fit for long-tailed citizen-science data—accurately capturing both single capture whales and heavily photo-documented resident individuals.

4. Developing the Algorithm: Vertical & Horizontal Repairing

Once the latent encounter parameters were established, we implemented a structured two-stage stochastic reallocation ("healing") algorithm to restructure the history matrix:

Stage 1: Vertical Thinning

Objective: Remove surplus ghosts within each primary occasion.

Using a system of simultaneous equations driven by the saddlepoint-fitted encounter distribution, surplus single-sighting histories are identified annually and "thinned" out, restoring the true annual sample totals ($n_t$) across the time series.

Stage 2: Horizontal Reallocation

Objective: Re-attach orphaned sightings across time.

Leftover "orphaned ghosts" still remain after stage 1 Vertical thinning has occurd. Individuals that have all their captures misidentified leave orphaned ghosts. Instead, joint likelihood weights are calculated across candidate multi-year individual histories, stochastically reallocating orphaned sightings back into existing true profiles.

Framework Performance
Bias Reduced from +7.2% to +0.05%

Benchmark testing on simulated multi-decade populations restored superpopulation ($N^*$) estimation to near-perfect accuracy without dropping valid observation data.

Thesis Takeaway

Through the stochastic reallocation process, misidentification bias in photo-ID datasets can be accounted for and corrected. A two-stage process that repairs yearly counts and individual life histories to statistically and biologically plauasible outcomes, repairs the data to mitigate the bias from the misidentification.
